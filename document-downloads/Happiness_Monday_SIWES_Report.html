CHAPTER ONE

INTRODUCTION

1.1 Background of SIWES

The Students’ Industrial Work Experience Scheme (SIWES) is a practical training programme intended to expose students in tertiary institutions to the realities of the workplace. It provides an opportunity to relate concepts taught in the classroom to professional tasks, organizational procedures, and industry expectations. Through supervised industrial attachment, students can develop technical competence, communication skills, work discipline, and a clearer understanding of their chosen field.

For a Mass Communication student, industrial training is particularly relevant because communication practice involves more than theoretical knowledge. It requires the ability to write for specific audiences, gather and verify information, monitor media coverage, prepare organizational messages, support public relations activities, and communicate responsibly across different platforms. An industrial placement allows students to observe these activities in a real organizational setting.

1.2 Objectives of SIWES

The general objective of SIWES is to bridge the gap between academic learning and practical work experience. The specific objectives relevant to this placement were:

• To gain practical experience in corporate communication and brand management.

• To apply principles of professional writing, public relations, media monitoring, and audience-focused communication.

• To understand how an organization coordinates internal communication, external communication, and event publicity.

• To develop workplace skills such as punctuality, teamwork, attention to detail, confidentiality, and accountability.

• To gain exposure to social media content preparation, event documentation, and media relations support.

• To identify professional strengths and areas requiring further development.

1.3 Scope and Importance of the Training

The training focused on activities carried out in the Corporate Communications and Brand Management Department of CIBN. The scope included daily media highlights, newspaper distribution, preparation of goodwill and congratulatory correspondence, media monitoring, photography and videography during stakeholder engagements, drafting and printing of internal documents, vendor-payment follow-up, social media support, and communication assistance for selected events and programmes.

The placement was important because it provided an opportunity to observe how communication contributes to an institution’s reputation, stakeholder relationships, public visibility, and internal coordination. It also demonstrated the importance of accuracy, consistency, timeliness, appropriate tone, and approval procedures in organizational communication.

1.5 Relevance of SIWES to Mass Communication

Mass Communication encompasses the production and dissemination of information through print, broadcast, digital, and organizational channels. In the workplace, communication practitioners may prepare written materials, monitor media coverage, support events, manage digital content, and maintain relationships with internal and external stakeholders. The CIBN placement offered practical exposure to several of these functions and helped demonstrate how communication principles are applied within a professional institution.

1.6 Roles of Stakeholders in SIWES

The effective implementation of SIWES involves cooperation among the student, the industrial organization, the institution, and the relevant SIWES coordinating bodies. The student is expected to observe workplace rules, complete assigned tasks, maintain a logbook, and prepare a report. The host organization provides supervised learning opportunities and workplace guidance. The university provides academic oversight and assesses the student’s learning outcomes.
